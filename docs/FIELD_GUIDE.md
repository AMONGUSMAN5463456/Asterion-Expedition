# Asterion Expedition · field guide

You are surveying the Asterion Reach, following the remains of an ancient
listening network. The story is told through your objective card, journal,
discoveries, and Signal Ruins. It advances as you explore; you can take detours
to collect materials, improve your equipment, and build a place to return to.

## Before you leave the landing site

Choose a comfortable mouse sensitivity in Settings. Arrow keys also turn your
view if you prefer keyboard steering. Look around slowly and
identify your ship, nearby plants and mineral clusters, and the pale shapes of
field structures. **WASD** moves you across the terrain. **Shift** sprints;
**Space** jumps and, held down, engages your jetpack. The energy gauge measures
your equipment reserve. Let it recover between long climbs. **Ctrl** brakes
you in the air; hold **Space + Ctrl** to stabilise a hover while energy remains.
Tap and release Space for a short jump. Buffered input makes jumps near a landing
or the edge of a ledge more forgiving. Solid trunks, rocks, and walls block your
body; small steps and accessible roofs can be crossed or stood on.

The location readout names your current planet and biome. The compass helps you
keep a heading. The central reticle identifies the thing you are aiming at;
the prompt beneath it tells you whether an action is available. Read the short
notices after a scan, purchase, craft, or launch—they explain what changed.

**Esc** pauses play and releases the mouse pointer. Inventory, fabrication,
navigation, construction, and journal panels also pause the expedition while
you read. You can take time to inspect costs without losing suit reserves.

## A practical opening route

1. **Collect 20 resource units.** Walk close to a stone cluster or plant and
   hold the left mouse button with it under your reticle. A beam and extraction
   meter show progress. Keep aiming until the collection notice appears. Start
   with ferrite and carbon; both remain useful throughout the expedition.
2. **Catalog three discoveries.** Aim at different plants, minerals, or roaming
   creatures and press **C**. The scanner records a target when one is in range.
   If your reticle has no target, it can pick a nearby unrecorded subject.
   Repeatedly scanning the same subject does not grant a new reward.
3. **Craft your first supply.** Press **K**, find Launch Cell, and check that
   you have five carbon and three ferrite. Craft one. A cell sits in cargo until
   used; it does not instantly spend itself on your ship.
4. **Check your reserves.** Open **I** or **Tab** to inspect cargo and use a
   needed item. Press **R** during play for a quick recharge chosen from your
   carried supplies. Keep some oxygen and sodium after crafting.
5. **Launch.** Walk back to the courier and press **E** or **F**. If you have
   wandered away, **X** recalls it to a nearby landing position. Look upward,
   hold **W**, and use **Space** to climb. The altitude readout is the height
   over local terrain. At 420 m, orbital insertion begins.
6. **Try a station approach.** In orbit, press **M** and choose the station's
   automatic approach. Close the panel if needed and let the ship travel. It
   opens the exchange when you arrive. Press **E** to cancel an approach if you
   want manual control. Sell spare materials, keep your essential supplies,
   and inspect the available contracts.
7. **Visit another world.** Use the system map to select an approach to a
   different planet. It brings you to a landing transition. Your new location
   is recorded in the journal and saved on arrival.

The objective card follows your progress. Actions already recorded can count
toward later cumulative goals, so exploration between chapters is useful.

## Surveying and gathering

Minerals, plants, and creatures have stable identities. New discoveries earn
credits and build your catalog. Scanner upgrades increase the value of your
survey work. Approach a subject and aim carefully when you want to record a
particular discovery instead of the scanner's nearest fallback target.

Mining uses the same reticle. Surface plants and mineral deposits must be
close enough for the beam to reach them. Asteroids can be mined at a longer
range while in orbit. The laser does not harvest during low-altitude flight;
land before gathering surface resources. Creatures are subjects for observation.

Every harvested deposit awards its full bundle. If you do not have enough free
cargo for that bundle, collection pauses and a notice reports the space needed.
The deposit is not depleted by a failed collection. Sell surplus, consume a
needed supply, craft a compact component, or install Cargo Lattice upgrades to
make room. Remember that crafted outputs also need storage space.

Mining a deposit removes it from that saved expedition. Moving away and coming
back does not restore a used deposit. Explore new terrain for fresh supplies.

## Suit care and a way home

| Gauge | What changes it | Common replenishment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen | Slowly decreases away from shelter | Oxygen or Life Gel; wait by a ship or outpost |
| Hazard | Planet conditions and storms use protection | Sodium or an Ion Cell; take shelter |
| Energy | Jetpack activity uses equipment power | Rest between climbs; an Ion Cell also restores energy |
| Shield | Runs down after environmental reserves are exhausted | Shelter, a Repair Kit, and expedition rescue |
| Fuel | Launching and powered travel use ship reserves | Launch Cells; orbital exchanges and outposts assist with service |

**R** chooses a useful recharge item from cargo and reports the result. Use the
individual cargo actions when you want control over which supply is consumed.
Unused consumables remain in cargo. The game refuses a recharge that has no
useful effect.

Weather changes with expedition time. A pleasant landing site can later become
stormy. Climate Weave upgrades reduce the rate at which weather consumes
protection. Your ship, nearby outposts, and field habitats are useful places to
pause a walk and restore suit reserves. Flight restores the suit's oxygen and
environmental protection.

The expedition is designed to recover from shortages. **X** recalls the ship
while on foot. The **Esc** menu offers rescue when supplies or circumstances
prevent a normal return. Rescue returns you to the current world's initial
landing area with your equipment and cargo, and restores all suit and ship
reserves. Rescue is free and remains available when you have no credits.

## Flying with confidence

There are two flight environments: **surface flight** above a terrain patch and
**orbit** between visible worlds. Your mouse turns the ship, **W** adds thrust,
and **S** brakes, then reverses. Arrow keys also steer. **A/D** strafe,
**Space** climbs, and **Ctrl**
descends. **Shift** boosts when power is available.

Flight assist is enabled by default and stabilises drift. In Settings you can
disable it to keep your momentum as the ship turns. **S** actively brakes in
either mode, then reverses. Camera motion can be disabled for a steady view;
field of view and mouse smoothing are also adjustable.

For a short surface excursion, launch, fly over the terrain, then release boost
and brake with **S**. Descend with **Ctrl** until altitude is below **65 m**.
Wait until speed is below **48 m/s**, then press **F**. The landing sequence
places your ship and returns you to walking nearby. Terrain is height based;
this is a forgiving landing system, not an aerodynamics simulator.

For orbit, climb to **420 m above the terrain**. The scene transition puts you
outside the planet. The new scale makes distances much larger. You can fly
manually, mine asteroids, dock at the station, or use navigation to automate a
trip. An approach is a short assisted journey, not a jump between star systems.

When flying manually toward a planet, press **F** once you are within its
approach distance. If you are too far away, the game tells you to continue
closer or use the map. The orbital atmosphere boundary prevents you from flying
through the sphere. Entry and landing use a separate scene transition. A manual
station visit uses **E** once the dock is in range.

The system map's automatic approach plots a clear route around planet and
asteroid collision volumes and takes care of steering and arrival. It
lands at a selected planet or opens the station exchange. **E** cancels it.
Your landing location is a prepared region of the selected world, so orbit is
not a geographic map of the terrain below.

## Trading and interstellar travel

Credits come from discoveries, story milestones, contracts, and selling cargo.
Outposts and orbital exchanges let you trade. The price shown in the panel is
the actual price used for that transaction. Prices vary by item and system;
buy and sell prices differ. Purchases still respect cargo capacity.

Keep a small reserve of core materials before selling. Ferrite, carbon,
oxygen, sodium, copper, and crystal support most early supplies. Gold and
archive fragments are valuable, but later fabrication may need them. A full
cargo hold can make a cheap purchase impossible; clear space first.

Travel between **star systems** uses a **Fold Cell**. Craft one from five
copper, three crystal, and four carbon, or buy supplies at an exchange. Use the
galaxy navigation actions while in orbit to choose a destination. A successful
fold consumes a cell and records an interstellar journey. Consuming a Fold
Cell as ordinary fuel also spends that item, so keep one in cargo for the next
system jump.

There are 24 named systems with four worlds each. The galaxy is deterministic:
a new expedition reaches the same named worlds and terrain seeds. Your
discoveries, mined deposits, built structures, and progression belong to your
own local save.

## Fabrication and equipment

**K** opens the Fabricator. Recipes show their material costs, outputs, and
availability. A successful fabrication removes its inputs and adds the listed
output as one transaction. Missing inputs or a full hold do not partially spend
your inventory. Upgrades are installed directly and do not occupy cargo as
unused equipment items.

| Equipment line | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Survey Laser | Extract deposits faster |
| Vector Pack | Improve lift and jetpack efficiency |
| Climate Weave | Explore difficult climates for longer |
| Cargo Lattice | Add room for resources, components, and supplies |
| Helix Drive | Improve powered flight efficiency |
| Deep Scanner | Improve survey work and discovery rewards |

Each line has three tiers. Earlier tiers lead toward more advanced recipes.
Choose equipment that solves the problem you are actually encountering: cargo
capacity if you keep returning with a full hold; mining if gathering feels
slow; environmental protection before a long survey on a harsher world.

Fabrication grows from raw resources into useful components. Ferrite and carbon
make alloy; copper and carbon make wire; wire, crystal, and cobalt make
circuits. Optical glass uses silicon and crystal. The full
[content reference](REFERENCE.md) lists exact quantities without requiring you
to keep a panel open.

## A field base

Open **B** on a planet to inspect construction options. Each building has a
listed material cost. A Survey Beacon is an inexpensive first landmark: ten
ferrite and four copper. Construction places an original prefab near your
position and records it on the current world.

Habitat, Solar Array, Mineral Extractor, and Lumen Garden support longer
expeditions. Use **COLLECT OUTPUT** in the construction panel to gather stored
yield. An extractor accumulates local ore, and a garden accumulates carbon;
adding a Solar Array doubles their production rate on that world. Production
uses active expedition time, including time spent exploring elsewhere. Each
producer stores up to 60 units, and uncollected output remains when cargo is
full. The remaining structures let you develop a recognizable
site: Cargo Vault, Landing Platform, Sky Observatory, Meridian Relay, and Path
Light. The vault is a visual supply landmark; it is not a second inventory.

Buildings appear 12 m ahead of you. Move between constructions to arrange an
outpost, and keep at least 9 m between structures. Obstructed or steep sites
are rejected without spending materials. Each world supports up to
48 expedition structures. Buildings use fixed designs and simple placement. There is no room-by-room
editor, terrain excavation, power-cable routing, or simulated settlement
population. Place a few structures with walking space between them. Their
records persist when you leave and return to the planet.

## Following the signal

The 18-stage story begins with gathering, cataloging, fabrication, and your
first launch. It then asks you to visit other worlds, read ruins, fold between
systems, install equipment, and establish a base. Later milestones reward a
wider catalog, more construction, and advanced archive fabrication.

Signal Ruins have a nearby interaction prompt. Approach and press **E** to
read the recovered record. A newly explored ruin yields an Archive Fragment;
leave a free cargo unit before investigating. Reopening the same ruin does not
produce another first-discovery reward. Several fragments are needed for the
Meridian Key, so consider keeping them instead of selling every find.

**J** gathers your objective, discoveries, and contract information. Contracts
give extra reasons to survey, mine, fabricate, and travel. Read each contract's
requirements and reward in the panel, then return to claim a completed job
through its available action. The story does not end free exploration: you can
continue to visit the remaining worlds, develop bases, and complete your
catalog afterward.

## Save, pause, and return

Press **F5** before finishing a session if you want an immediate checkpoint.
The game also autosaves after a minute of active play, on planet arrival, and
when quitting normally. **F9** opens a confirmation before reloading the saved
expedition. Reloading discards changes since that save.

The title screen's Continue action restores your slot. Starting a fresh
expedition uses that same slot when saved, so copy the save folder first if you
want to keep an earlier journey. Save paths are listed in the main
[README](../README.md). Close the game before copying, restoring, or editing
save files. A backup is maintained for recovery from an invalid primary file.

A low-altitude flight save deliberately resumes on foot beside your parked
ship. An orbit save resumes in orbit. This keeps a restored expedition from
starting with an uncontrolled landing. Your overall cargo, catalog, and story
progress continue from the saved state.
